Packaging Strategies Editorial Team,

Packaging Strategies' Submissions Process Guidelines invite non-biased web-exclusive articles of 500–750 words for packaging readers, and the site's editorial coverage specifically includes flexible packaging, films and coatings, and form-fill-seal/filling and closing. This proposal is tailored to that format and audience.

The article would help food and beverage packaging teams avoid a common specification problem: selecting a pouch, laminate and spout as separate items even though they must work as a system. It would organize the decision around six practical questions:

1. What are the product's viscosity, oil, acidity and particle characteristics?
2. What shelf-life and oxygen, moisture, aroma or light barriers are actually required?
3. Will the pack be ambient-filled, hot-filled or processed under another temperature regime?
4. Where should the spout sit, and how will its diameter affect filling and consumer use?
5. What sealing window and machine-handling constraints must the laminate meet?
6. Which filled-pack and line trials should be completed before commercial production?

The conclusion would give readers a concise handoff checklist covering product, fill weight, pouch dimensions, structure, spout position, cap, filling method, storage conditions and test criteria. The article would avoid supplier comparisons, superlatives and promotional claims. It would also distinguish starting-point structures from final specifications that must be validated for the actual product and process.
